We are not asking you to risk your money and wait in line for it. Every profitable month sends half of the distributable profit to you alone, and keeps doing it until every reais you put in has come home. Only after that does anyone split anything evenly.

One legal note, so nothing is a surprise at the notary: Brazilian law does not permit a partner to be excluded from losses altogether (Código Civil, art. 1.008). The contrato social therefore allocates losses disproportionately to the investing partner as far as the law allows, and limited liability caps every partner's exposure at the capital they subscribed. The practical effect is exactly what is described above — nobody but you is ever asked for money — but the wording belongs to a lawyer of your choosing.

Second floor · the deal

Upstairs is not a partnership.

Expensive shows are a different trade with a different rhythm, and folding them into the company would blur both. So the room is programmed by two people who are paid for what they sell, and who own nothing.

Who

Eduardo Senna manages artists and opens the doors to touring acts. Rodrigo Tavares books the calendar. They work as contracted bookers for the room, not as partners in anything.

A percentage of that show's net box office, paid on settlement. This is the part that rewards selling rather than merely booking, and it is where their real money is. Also indicative and open to discussion.

Approval before booking

No show is confirmed without a budget approved by the house: artist fee, production, sound, marketing and projected ticketing. A cap on the artist fee is set annually, and anything above it needs a second signature. This is the gate that keeps an ambitious booking from becoming the house's problem.

Who carries a show that fails

The house does, and in cash terms that means you do, on the same terms as downstairs. The bookers lose their commission on a show that does not sell but keep their fee, which is why the approval gate above is not a formality and why the annual cap on artist fees matters more than any other number upstairs. Paying on commission buys simplicity and costs risk transfer, and we would rather you read that here than discover it after a bad Saturday.
